Definitions and data
Organized violence deaths
UCDP best estimate, summed over state-based conflict, non-state conflict and one-sided violence, by country of location. Covers 1989–2025.
Country in conflict
A country-year with at least 25 deaths from organized violence, the same threshold as the incidence variable in the analysis.
Conflicts and wars
UCDP/PRIO armed conflict v26.1: state-based conflicts with at least 25 battle deaths in the year; wars have 1,000 or more.
US as a party
The US (Gleditsch–Ward code 2) appears as side A or B (primary) or as a secondary warring party supporting one side with troops. In the heatmap the US counts as a party in a country when that country is among the conflict's locations.
US sanction cases
GSDB V4 cases with the US among the senders, counted in the year they start, without ICC case 685, the same shock as the US local projections. GSDB ends in 2023. UN cases are not counted as US cases, even where the US enforced them.
US sanction in force
The panel's us_sanction flag: at least one GSDB case with the US among the senders is active against the country that year. A new case is counted only in its start year and only for the state GSDB names as target.
Bosnia and the other Yugoslav successors
The 1991 US arms embargo on Yugoslavia (case 422) is dated to Yugoslavia, because Bosnia-Herzegovina enters the state system only in 1992. Following the project's attribution rules, Bosnia, Croatia, Slovenia and Macedonia carry it as an arms embargo in force until 1996, so Bosnia shows a US sanction in force in 1992–96 but no new US case. The trade sanctions of the Bosnian war (UN case 470, 1992) targeted Belgrade, which UCDP codes as a secondary party backing the Bosnian Serbs. UCDP never codes the US as a party to the Bosnian conflict. The 1999 NATO campaign appears under Serbia (Kosovo).
