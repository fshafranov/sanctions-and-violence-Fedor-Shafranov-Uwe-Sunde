Conflict dynamics and US involvement, 1989–2025

Which conflicts dominated each period, how often the US fought in them, and where it imposed sanctions. Organized violence from UCDP (state-based, non-state and one-sided), conflicts from UCDP/PRIO v26.1, US sanction cases from GSDB V4 without ICC case 685, as in the local projections.

The four periods at a glance

Sanction coverage counts every US case in force, not only new ones. Change in deaths compares the target's deaths the year before a US case starts with two years after, for targets with at least 25 deaths the year before, one count per country-year. It is descriptive, with no counterfactual.

Definitions and data
Organized violence deaths
UCDP best estimate, summed over state-based conflict, non-state conflict and one-sided violence, by country of location, 1989–2025.
Country in conflict
A country-year with at least 25 deaths from organized violence, the threshold of the incidence variable in the analysis.
Conflicts and wars
UCDP/PRIO armed conflict v26.1: state-based conflicts with at least 25 battle deaths in the year; wars have 1,000 or more.
US as a party
The US (Gleditsch–Ward code 2) is side A or B (primary) or a secondary warring party with troops. A country counts when it is among the conflict's locations.
US sanction cases
GSDB V4 cases with the US among the senders, counted in their start year for the state GSDB names as target, without ICC case 685. UN cases are not counted as US cases, even where the US enforced them. GSDB ends in 2023.
US sanction in force
The panel's us_sanction flag: at least one GSDB case with the US among the senders is active against the country that year.
Bosnia and the other Yugoslav successors
The 1991 US arms embargo on Yugoslavia (case 422) is dated to Yugoslavia, since Bosnia-Herzegovina enters the state system in 1992. Under the project's attribution rules Bosnia, Croatia, Slovenia and Macedonia carry it as an arms embargo in force until 1996, so Bosnia shows a US sanction in force in 1992–96 but no new US case. The trade sanctions of the Bosnian war (UN case 470, 1992) targeted Belgrade, which UCDP codes as a secondary party backing the Bosnian Serbs. UCDP never codes the US as a party to the Bosnian conflict; the 1999 NATO campaign appears under Serbia (Kosovo).
